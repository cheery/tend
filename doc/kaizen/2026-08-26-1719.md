*Written because the lamp lit on the study commit.  Henri: "record that
idea doesn't fly", then "Lets look at spec/os.md and check which ones
are already satisfied and which ones are in the air?  And which ones

## What the sitting was

Two things.  The toolbox verdict recorded on `work-environment-ai`:
the *enforcement* is withdrawn (a list is not a boundary; the
install-test "why" is the manifest's alone; the set is not fixed for a
session), and only the manifest half survives, serving os.md property 2.
Then a status pass over the sixteen properties and three open problems
(`doc/os-status-2026-08-26.md`): each placed in one of Henri's three
buckets — satisfied, simple-solution-here, in-the-air — grounded in a
real mechanism.

## What went right

**The withdrawal kept the true part.**  Henri said the idea does not
fly; the honest record is not "dead" but "the enforcement does not
hold, the manifest does" — flattening it would have lost the half that
serves property 2.  Recording a rejection at its true grain.

**The study surfaced two things worth seeing.**  Open problem 1 — how
are users' data protected from programs — is *closed* as of today, one
of only three real problems on the 2026-08-19 sheet.  And the in-the-air
items cluster in exactly two layers (the language/verification future,
and the store + restore-latency layer), which is a roadmap, not a
scatter.  `llama-server`'s 30 s cold load was named as open problem 3
arriving in the flesh.

## What went wrong, named as the session's

**Nothing measured wrong.**  The one risk in a status pass is crediting
a stance as a built thing; guarded by marking 3, 11, 12 as "a stance,
in force" rather than "done", and splitting 4 and 6 into their
satisfied and in-the-air halves rather than calling either whole.
