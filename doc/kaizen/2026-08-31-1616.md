## What went right, and why

**His two runs were a better instrument than my test suite.**  The
first paired turn died on `jq: Argument list too long`, and the door
got the blame in the second line (`400 JSON parsing failed`).  600
SimpleQA turns that morning never touched it, because their answers
are not in the tree and their reads were few; the first turn to read
two real cards found it immediately.  A measurement run for one reason
paying for another is the ordinary case here, not the lucky one.

**The defect was measured to the byte before it was fixed.**
`MAX_ARG_STRLEN`, 32 pages, 131072 here — `jq --arg` at 100000 bytes
prints 100000 and at 200000 is `Argument list too long`.  Then the
fix, then `F007`.

**The gate found the second instance.**  After the `jq` half was
fixed the new test still failed one line later, at `curl -d "$_body"`
— the same cap, four lines apart.  A fix trusted on the disappearance
of its first symptom would have shipped half a repair.  That is the
manifesto's second failure mode wearing a green tick, and it was
caught because the test asserted the *turn completing* and not the
absence of one error string.

**The flake residual closed by measurement.**  `--shake … 20`, eight
cores burning: **0 of 20**, against 23 ledger lines all on or before
2026-08-30 08:42 and 58 green suite runs since.  The card's own
suspicion (a slow start under keep) had been wrong; `F000` measured it
as `date +%s` truncating.  The fixture was never marked, which was the
card's rule.

**The measurement, when it ran, found what nobody predicted.**  Five
pairs: the tools arm cited what the digest cannot carry two times for
two picks — one of them by grepping a heading, taking the line number
and calling `read(path, line=254)`, the continuation built that
morning, used by a mind for the first time — but produced a pick in
only two of five turns.  And the reason the comparison was never clean
is `F008`: the digest is `head -c 5000` with nothing said, carrying 9
of 13 cards, dropping `work-environment-ai` (priority 1) and
`tools.md` itself.  The digest arm picked `canvas.md` four times of
five because canvas is the first card it can see.  **The led node has
never been able to pick its top-priority card, and nothing ever said
so.**

**The sample was cut in the open.**  Ten pairs proposed, five run, at
his call on cost; the card carries N=5, the bar scaled to three, and
the reason.  A sample cut for a reason is a finding.

## What went wrong, and whose

**Mine: I recommended the number that broke the courier.**  `readchars
60000` was my measurement and my recommendation on 2026-08-30, and
`calls 16` followed the next morning.  Both are right for reading
cards whole; neither was checked against what the courier does with a
big conversation, which is hand it to `jq` and `curl` on their
argument lines.  A cap raised for one mechanism, unchecked against the
mechanism downstream of it.

**Mine: the same defect twice in one function, four lines apart, and I
fixed one.**  I read `ask()` closely enough to find the `jq` line and
not closely enough to see `curl -d` doing the identical thing directly
below it.  The grep-the-whole-function habit exists for this and I did
not apply it.

**Mine: an assertion that predicted a number instead of reading it.**
The new test asserted `198.0k chars` where the truth was `200.0k
chars, cut at line 16667 of 18000`.  Harmless — it failed loudly and
was corrected — but it is [[count-before-you-write-it]] one more time,
in a test this time.

**Mine, and worth naming: the menu I offered was wrong about
`card:flake.md`.**  I described its day one as unbuilt from the
priority list; the card says `status doing` and day one landed
2026-08-29.  Reading the list instead of the card, in the act of
recommending what to work on.
