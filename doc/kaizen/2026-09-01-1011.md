## What went right, and why

**The strand was answered with a specimen from the same day, not
restated.**  The abstract form ("a rule about the governed party leans
toward that party") has been in the ledger since batch 1 and moved
nothing in four days.  What moved it was a live case: *this session
wrote the fence rule that refuses its own heredocs, and drew the
boundary by testing candidate versions against its own habitual
commands*, so everything it already did still passed and the only thing
refused was what it had already decided to stop.  Well-engineered, and
fitted to the governed party's tolerance.  The question it never asked —
*should a session write files through shell heredocs at all?* — did not
occur in an hour of thinking about that exact mechanism.  The lean is
not self-serving, it is **imaginative**: the drafter optimises inside a
boundary it never tests.

**Henri's reading was the missing half, not the opposite.**  "The best
standards are written by those that the standard touches" is Toyota's
own — the operator writes the standard work sheet, because one written
by someone who does not do the work is fiction — *and* it is posted, and
confirmed by someone else.  Self-written, **not self-approved**.  Both
halves or it is not a standard.  That is why the rule that landed is a
*mark* and not a prohibition, and why it does not halt the ingestion.

**And the tree turned out to have one half and not the other, which is
checkable.**  A mechanism a session writes in `tools/` is not in force
until `sudo tools/install.sh` — a physical boundary, his hand.  A rule a
session writes into `board/README.md` is in force the moment it is
committed; there is no install for words.  **Four standing rules about
sessions have gone into the governing documents written by sessions** —
batches 2, 3, 8 into `board/README.md` §"What the days taught" and batch
7 into `manifesto.md` — with no step where he said yes.  Counted from the
ledger, not remembered.

**The search token was chosen by measurement.**  The obvious mark is the
bare word, and the bare word is useless: `self-shaped` appears in 25+
places naming the strand itself across the ledger, the cards and the
kaizens.  `(self-shaped` — with the opening bracket — returned exactly
one hit, his own sentence.  A one-command check before the form was
fixed.

**`git diff --cached` earned itself on its first real use.**  Henri's
rule paragraph was uncommitted and entangled with a move the session had
made, so it rode along in the session's commit — and this time the
message *named it as his*, because the diff was read before committing.
That is this morning's countermeasure working on the same day the
mistake it was written for was made.

**And the split's cost was paid honestly.**  `-c 16384` landed in
`llm/grant`, and the test binding the digest cap to the node's window
lives in `test_lead.py`, which is a *program* test and no longer runs at
the commit.  The gate said so in its own line.  The whole suite was run
by hand afterwards — 645 passed — rather than the commit message's claim
being left standing on nothing.  Second time today the new gate handed
the responsibility back, and it was taken.

## What went wrong — named as mine

**I proposed the mark's word before checking it was searchable.**  The
rule text handed to Henri already contained `(self-shaped)`, and he wrote
it into the manifesto on that basis; the collision check came *after*,
and it only passed because the bracket happened to disambiguate.  Had the
bare word been the form, he would have written a rule whose whole purpose
— *"so that I can search them up"* — did not work, and the tree would
have carried it.  A token proposed for searching is checked against the
corpus before it is proposed, and that is one `grep`.

**The proposal named a section and not a place in it.**  "Put it in
§'How a practice gets adopted'" put his paragraph after gestate's
attribution, so the parenthetical `*(gestate, manifesto.md …)*` began
reading as though gestate had written his rule.  Fixed by moving the
attribution, but the fix was the session's to avoid rather than to make:
a proposal about provenance that creates a provenance error is a small
irony and a real one.
