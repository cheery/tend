## What the second half was

Two cards and a defect.  `card:swe-bench.md` opened at the shape whose
subject is *this tree* rather than a model; `card:session-program.md`
brought current after four days in which every sitting went to the door;
`F013` found by trying to run its measurement and fixed at three call
sites.  Then Henri's last two arms landed and falsified a hypothesis this
session had written ninety minutes earlier.

## What went right

**The falsified hypothesis is the best thing in this batch.**  At 15:2x a
session wrote, from the head arm hitting its cap in 10 of 24, that
`readchars 4000` with more calls "is the obvious next one".  Run at 32
calls it went 22/24 against 24/24 (p = 0.49) and used *fewer* calls —
10.9 against 12.6 — with zero arms capped.  **Handed twice the budget it
took less.**  Hitting a cap and being hurt by a cap are different things
and the session read the first as the second.

Thirty cents, and it closed a plausible line that would otherwise have
sat in `card:tools.md` as a standing next step for weeks.  This is what
`card:questions.md`'s `measure` category is *for*: the session wrote a
number and a cost instead of a hunch, and the hunch died in twenty
minutes.

**`F013` was found by using a tool, not auditing one.**  `launch.sh llm
check` was run to see whether the gemma4 arm could go, and it said the
node was unrunnable and the install broken — four false sentences.  The
three-verdict rule promoted this morning had named exactly where its next
face would come from ("every other `--check` in the tree"), and it came
from the file the rule cites, eleven lines above the mechanism it names,
six hours after Henri approved it.  **A promoted rule that says where to
look next is worth more than one that describes what happened.**

**And the fix's own third face.**  With the grant paths repaired the
check still said `NOT installed`; the survivor was the loader failing on
libraries under `/opt/intel/oneapi`, which the line directly below it
already said the fence hides.  Read the whole output after a fix, not the
line you aimed at.

## Wrong, mine

**I ran a full suite over files I was still editing** — two reds on
`test_fixme.py::F013`, both passing alone, because the suite read the
entry between its `status` changing and its `git mv`.  Two lines on the
failure ledger describing a tree that never existed.

**Second time in one afternoon, by the opposite door.**  At 14:5x it was
`compare.py` edited while 48 arms ran through it.  The rule written after
that one — *never edit an instrument a measurement is running through* —
did not catch this because a test suite did not look like a measurement.
It is one.  The corrected rule is broader and is the day's real lesson:
**a measurement and its subject must both be still.**

**And the F012 fix cost a comparability I had not counted.**  It landed
between arms 2 and 3 deliberately, so the last two arms would record
calls run rather than attempted — and that made the `calls` field mean
different things across the four arms.  Recoverable (the `C:` lines carry
it, and the analysis recomputed all four uniformly), but it was traded
without being noticed at the time, and "I fixed it first so the new data
is clean" quietly means "the old and new data now disagree on a field".

## What should change tomorrow

1. **A measurement and its subject must both be still.**  Twice today by
   opposite doors; the narrow version of this rule was written after the
   first and let the second through.
2. **Changing a field mid-experiment trades one comparability for
   another** — say which, at the time, not in the analysis.
3. Standing and untouched: an approved rule cannot accrue its own
   evidence (the three-verdict rule predicted `F013` and the proof had to
   live in `fixme/resolved/F013.md`).  Henri's to judge.

## The whole day, since this closes it

Eleven commits, 13:48 to 16:13.  Two mechanisms built and bound to each
other, two cards opened, three F-numbers opened and three closed, 96 arms
of Henri's money, one day-old finding falsified by a measurement sized to

**Every error was one shape** — a sentence written before anything was

result, and the criterion fixed before the data could choose it.
