## What went right

**His answer was better than the session's proposal, and the difference
is the whole experiment.**  Asked what "cold" means, a session had
proposed stripping the conditioning prose — no README, no manifesto, no
kaizen.  That confounds the rules with the reasoning behind them: a model
missing both could differ for either reason.  His arm keeps the rules and
removes only the *why*.

So the variable becomes exactly what this tree bets on.  Every rule here
cites the incidents that earned it; every correction sits beside the
original; every promotion names the kaizens behind it.  **His control
asks whether that is load-bearing, or whether "do X" would have done the
same work.**  A session defining its own control would have compared
*conditioned* against *absent* — a comparison the tree wins by
construction — which is precisely why the question was marked `his call`
and not answered by the party it measures.

**The mechanism routed it correctly on its first afternoon.**  The
question was written where it arose, marked `his call`, reached him
through `keeper.md`'s grep, and came back with something a session could
not have produced.  That is `card:questions.md`'s day one doing the one
thing it was built for, five hours old.

**Writing the arm produced findings before running it.**  Three rules
lost something real in translation and `doc/cold/notes.md` says which —
the self-shaped rule keeps its command and loses the sentence that makes
anyone comply; the fixture rule keeps a plain form that took five
incidents to reach; go-and-see keeps the instruction and loses the story
about why a fluent explanation is the enemy.  Three verdicts may be the
counter-example: it seems to need no story at all.

And **two rules would not become commands.**  "Imposed, tolerated, owned"
and "the best standards are written by those the standard touches" are
not instructions in any form.  They were dropped rather than mangled, and
that is a result the arm produced before it ran: *some of what this tree
carries has no imperative form.*

**The draft's four uncertainties went to him rather than being decided.**
Length as a confound, one file or a tree, whether grouping by subject
makes the cold arm *better organised* than the real thing, and whether a
model arm and a session arm want one artifact.  Each is a design decision
about the control, which is the same reason the definition was his.

## Wrong, mine

**"What ended up in line for next session" was answered with a plan, and
he named it: *"that's autocomplete proposing."*  He is right.**  He asked
what was in line; the honest answer is the board's priority plus the two
marks waiting on him.  Instead the reply put the commandment tree first
and called it "first work", which is a session ordering the board — and
`board/README.md` says the tiebreak between two workable cards is his.
He took it anyway and said why, so nothing was lost; but he had to notice
it, which means the reply had already made the choice look like a
finding.

The tell was there in the wording and went unread: *"First work, and it
comes from your answer"* asserts an ordering, where *"here is what is
open, and here is what I would pick"* offers one.  One sentence apart, and
only the second leaves the tiebreak where it belongs.
