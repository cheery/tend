## What the sitting was

`card:self.md` — the fence guards the settings but not the scripts that
enforce them, measured writable from inside the fence.  Then the leash
demonstration, asked for with `bus` open, which came back `plain`: the

## What went right

**The question was answered by measuring, not by moving files.**  Henri
asked for a directory; the honest answer was a protected set, and the
thing that made it honest was `test -w` from inside the fence showing
`sandbox.sh` writable.  A folder would have hidden the hole; the card
names it.

**The demonstration failed informatively.**  Opening `bus` and running
the leash was meant to prove the cpu fix; instead it proved the leash
cannot reach scope mode inside the fence at all.  That is worth more
than the proof would have been: it settles `grant`'s architecture (the
leash outside the fence) by trying the inside and watching it degrade.

**The bus row was found not to deliver a bus** — socket absent, DBUS
unset — before anything relied on it.  A row that silently does nothing
is exactly what `vision.md` forbids; it is now on a card, not a
surprise waiting.

## What went wrong, named as the session's

**The bus row was written on 2026-08-25 without a `--check` probe, so
it claimed a reach it did not deliver.**  `sandbox.sh --check` tests
the default rows and not the optional ones; a row is a reach, and an
untested reach is a `plain`-shaped lie in the making.  The next time a
row is added, `--check` gets a probe for it in the same commit.

**Time.**  This sitting ran to 102 minutes of a 120 limit chasing the
bus down; the finding was reachable in ten and the rest was the session
not accepting `plain` as the answer soon enough.  The ledger line said
`plain` at minute one; the session read it as a bug to fix rather than
a fact to record for two more probes.
