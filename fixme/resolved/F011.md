## Why this is the defect and the model's behaviour is not

That qwen3.8-max stops emitting a reasoning channel when tools are in
the request may be a bug, a documented limitation, or a deliberate
choice on someone else's side.  This tree cannot fix it and does not
claim to know which it is.

What is tend's, and is wrong, is that **the account is a record and it
recorded something that did not happen.**  `card:tools.md`'s whole
measurement rests on those accounts; the next reader comparing a
thinking run against a non-thinking run would have found six files all
saying `thinking on` and no way to see that half of them had no
thinking in them.  The finding that made this visible was found by
grepping the raw `replies` for `T:` lines, which is not something an
account should require.

It is the same shape as `F008` and `F010` one more time: a mechanism did
something other than what it said, and the saying is the part that was
missing.  Here it is worse than a cap, because a cap that says nothing
is silent and this one **asserts**.

## What would make this entry wrong

If `reasoning:{enabled:true}` is being honoured and the model simply
chose to emit nothing into that channel on those three turns, then
"thinking on" is a true statement about the request and the account is
right.  That is a real possibility and it is why the `shows` line says
*the model sent no thinking* rather than *thinking was off*.  But it
does not rescue the account: on either reading, a reader wants to know
that the channel came back empty, and no account says so.  The fix is
the same under both readings.

## Resolved 2026-09-01 — and the table above was three arms short

`tools/compare.py` records the fact now.  `_parse_replies` keeps the `T:`
line it had been discarding — including its continuation lines, which
carry the reasoning channel's own newlines exactly as the answer's do —
and `_thinking_line(asked, thought)` writes one of three things into the
account, never the flag:

    thinking off — the node's own condition
    thinking asked on, and 8370 chars of reasoning came back
    thinking asked on, and NO reasoning came back — the model answered in the content channel

A turn that never asked and a turn that asked and got nothing are
different turns, and the gate holds that they cannot read the same.

**The entry's own `seen` line was incomplete, and Henri corrected it.**
It recorded six arms, all qwen.  There were **nine**, across **three
models** — the last three were run while this entry was being written,
and the successful one was `tencent/hy3` and not qwen, which the entry's
author had assumed.  Replayed through the fix over the real records:

| model | arm | calls | pick | what the account now says |
|---|---|---|---|---|

| qwen/qwen3.8-max | tools | 7 | — | **NO reasoning came back** |

**Nine arms, four with a reasoning channel, four with a pick — the same
four.**  That correlation is why this was worth fixing rather than
noting: the account was hiding the only variable that has predicted
anything about a pick turn, and it was hiding it behind a line that
asserted the opposite.

**`tools/deliver.sh` was left alone, deliberately.**  It already prints a
`T:` line when reasoning arrives and omits it when none does, so its
record carries the truth; what it cannot do is distinguish "thinking was
never asked for" from "thinking was asked for and nothing came", because
it does not write the negative.  The reader that knows the flag is
`compare.py`, and that is where the fix belongs.  If a second reader of
these records ever needs the distinction from the raw file alone, that is
a new entry and one more line in `deliver.sh`.
