## Why it matters more than a truncation usually would

The digest is the *whole* of what a led node knows about the board.  A
cut card is not a shortened card: it is a card that does not exist for
the mind being asked to choose.  So the node's choice has been made
from a board that silently ends at `silent-cord`, and every judgement
about what the node picks — including "does it need tools" — has been
made against that.  `card:tools.md`'s prediction says the tooled turn
cites what the digest cannot carry; it turns out the digest cannot
carry four entire cards, which is a stronger and less flattering thing
than the card imagined.

It also touches the tree's own rule.  A cap is a gate and the tree
says so (`card:tools.md` §"Short prompts": *a cap is a gate, not a
habit*), but a gate that fires without a word is the failure this
whole project is built against — `manifesto.md`'s one sentence is that
being wrong has to be visible.  The `readchars` cut says `[… cut at N
chars, at line L of T; read(path, line=L) continues]`.  This one says
nothing at all.

## Shapes, none picked — the person's call

- **(a) Say what was dropped.**  Keep the cap; append one line —
  *4 cards did not fit: simpleqa.md, sitting-everywhere.md, tools.md,
  work-environment-ai.md* — so the mind knows its board is partial and
  can ask.  Smallest, and it makes the defect visible rather than
  absent.
- **(b) Fit the board instead of cutting it.**  Give each card an
  equal share of the budget (`ctxchars / n`) so all thirteen appear
  shortened, rather than nine whole and four gone.  A board is a list;
  a truncated list is a different list.
- **(c) Order by the priority, not the alphabet.**  The board's own
  order is in `board/README.md`; cutting the *bottom* of the priority
  list is at least defensible, where cutting the alphabet's tail is
  arbitrary.  Costs a parse of the README.
- **(d) Raise the cap.**  Cheapest to type and the worst answer alone:
  the number is gemma's 8 k context and the whole point of the digest
  is that it fits a small mind.  Raising it hides the shape of the
  problem for exactly as long as the board is short.

(a) and (b) compose, and (a) alone is worth landing on its own: it
turns a silent loss into a stated one, which is the tree's standing
answer to every cap it has.

## What would make this entry wrong

If the node is never asked to lead again — the digest exists for
`tools/lead.sh`, and if the door's tooled turn replaces it entirely
(`card:tools.md`'s day two) then the digest is dead code and this is a
note in its obituary rather than a defect.  That is a real
possibility and the reason this entry does not pick a shape: the
measurement that decides it is the same one that found this.

## Resolved 2026-09-01 — (d) and (a), at Henri's "do d and a"

**What the shapes were worth, corrected.**  The entry called (d)
"cheapest to type and the worst answer alone: the number is gemma's 8 k
context".  That was wrong about the number, and the correction is the
whole cause: **5000 was never gemma's 8 k context.**  It was chosen for
the node at `-c 2048`, and `37092d7` took the node to `-c 8192` on
2026-08-28 without touching it.  So (d) was not "raise the cap past what
fits" — it was *"the cap has been four days behind the window it was
sized for, and nothing in the tree reads one against the other."*  That
is yesterday's own item 4 (`doc/kaizen/2026-08-31-1616.md`): a number
that fits one mechanism and is silently wrong for the next.

**Measured before it was chosen.**  From the model's own GGUF header:
`gemma4.context_length = 262144`, 30 layers in a `[local ×5, global]`
pattern, 8 KV heads on the local layers pinned to a 1024 window and 2 on
the five global ones.  The window the node is *given* is `-c 8192`.  The
whole open board is **7516 characters** — about 2500 tokens — so it has
fitted in the node's head since 2026-08-28 and nobody knew.

**(d)**: `TEND_CTXCHARS` 5000 → **20000**, which is ~6700 tokens at a
pessimistic 3 chars/token, leaving ~1100 of the 8192 for the prompt's
framing and the 160-token reply.  Today's board fits 2.6 times over.
The number is not the point; the *gate* is —
`test_the_cap_fits_the_window_the_node_actually_has` parses `-c` out of
`llm/grant` and goes red if either end moves without the other, so this
particular staleness cannot recur silently.

**(a)**: the cut is on a card boundary, whole cards only, and once one
card is dropped the rest are — what the node sees is a prefix of the
board and never a gap in the middle.  When anything is dropped the
digest ends with

    [N cards did not fit: a.md, b.md.  The board is longer than this list; these
    cards exist and are not shown.  Pull the cord if the one you want is missing.]

which is what `readchars` does and this did not.  The last clause is
deliberate: the node has a cord, and "the card I need is not here" is
exactly what it is for.

**And the second copy was gated.**  `tools/compare.py` carried its own
`out[:DIGEST_CHARS]` — `head -c` in Python, silent the same way — and
its module docstring claimed it "builds the same digest lead.sh builds"
with nothing checking it.  Both ends of this fix had to be written
twice.  The new test runs the real `lead.sh` against a stub and compares
what actually reached the model against `compare.digest()`, whole and
cut; it was shown red on a one-character wording drift before it was
trusted.

**What this did not fix, measured and left standing: `F009`.**  Each
card's `because` is cut at `head -8` with nothing said, and 9 of the 13
open cards are cut by it — `private.md` loses 14 lines of its 22.  That
is the same defect one level down and it was not among the four shapes.
The whole board with every `because` entire is 12088 characters, 60 % of
the new budget, so lifting it is now affordable; whether the node should
read whole `because`s is a judgement about what the mind is given, not a
cap that is wrong, and it is Henri's.
