## Measured, 2026-08-30 09:15–09:40 — at Henri's "F001" (the round's first question)

**After F000's clock fix, 5 of 10** under the shake — down from 8, so
the skipping clock was part of it and not all of it.  One run under
eight burners, with F000's `$STATE/ticks` instrument, the full-burn
fixture at `idle 2`:

    1788069656 8 7 214 38 1 1
    1788069657 9 8 176  0 2 2    the CPU column FELL, 214 → 176, to the window's base

A CPU count cannot fall.  176 is `base_ticks` — the fallback the read
took when `/proc/$pid/stat` could not be opened.  The program ran its
eight seconds and exited during the watch's `sleep 1`; the shell reaped
it there; the next tick could not read its stat, took the fallback,
saw no progress, and — two ticks after the last busy one — wrote
`idle: nothing has pulled n for 2s`.  Then `[ -n "$why" ] && rc=0`
masked the exit code, and the death notice (`card:canvas.md` day two,
the line `tools/panel.py` reads) was never written, because a zero
exit writes none.  **On the llm node this is a llama-server crash
between two ticks recorded as "idle", with nothing on the person's
side** — the exact blindness the death notice was built to end, open
under it since 2026-08-29.

**The fix**: an unreadable stat, or a zombie (state `Z` — a program
exited and not yet collected, which `kill -0` still says is alive),
ends the watch; `wait` says what it was.  Idleness is never judged on
a program that is gone or going.

**The other half was the fixture**, as with F000's burst test: under
eight burners a full burn gets ~28 ticks a second (the run above), and
`idle 2` asks for 50 in two ticks — a margin under one tick, so the
test failed by the rule's letter on a box that could not give it a
core.  It says `idle 4` now, as the burst fixture does; the claim ("a
program burning a core is not idle") is unchanged.  0 of 10 under the
shake.

**The gate builds the exit's side**: `/bin/sh -c 'sleep 1.5; …; exit
3'` under `idle 2` — alive at tick 1, gone at tick 2.  Against HEAD's
launcher (`b25e548`, F000 fixed, this not): `idle: nothing has pulled
exitnode for 2s`, exit 0, no death notice.  Against this one: `exited
3: exitnode stopped by itself`, exit 3, the andon record's line
carrying "prog: gave up".  Deterministic at ordinary load; under a
load heavy enough that tick 1 lands after the exit, the old launcher
passes it by accident and this one still passes — a gate that never
reds wrongly.
