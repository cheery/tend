## Measured, 2026-08-30 08:30–09:10 — at Henri's "do the F000 fix if it's appropriate to do now"

**The ledger's `wall` is the suite's, not the test's** (`tools/suite.py`
`run()`), so 210–234 s said nothing about the failure; the assertion had
never been read (kaizen 2048: the gate's output was filtered both
times).  By hand, 20 runs at load ~1: **3 of 20**, each one the idle
stop, the run ~6.5 s.

**The instrument first, then the reading.**  `tools/launch.sh` now
writes `$STATE/ticks`, one line a tick — the clock, seconds since
start, the tick, the program's CPU ticks, ticks since the window's
base, the window's age and the ticks since the program was last found
busy — cleared at each `run`.  The failed run, the 30 % burster at
`idle 2`:

    1788068106 5 168 33 1 1
    1788068107 6 202 67 2 2    busy: 67 ticks since base; base reset
    1788068109 8 202  0 2 2    the clock read 2 s later; since-busy 2 ≥ idle: stopped

Between those two ticks ~1.02 s passed.  `date +%s` truncates, so a
tick that begins at x.99 and ends at (x+2).01 reads as two seconds;
the window is declared in seconds and measured on an integer clock,
and can be a whole second short.  A 30 %-of-a-core program crosses the
half-core-second threshold every ~1.7 s, so on any *odd* tick that
straddles, the 2 s window has run out — not the first window's slide,
every tick.  The suspected cause had the mechanism wrong and the
family right.

**The fix: count the window in ticks of the watch loop.**  Each tick
is a `sleep 1` or longer, so IDLE ticks are never fewer than IDLE
seconds — the direction that errs toward running, which is
`card:flake.md`'s whole lesson (a loaded box must not read a busy
program as idle).  `busy_tick`, `base_tick` replace `busy`, `base_at`;
the pulse and the sitting stay on the wall clock (they are the person's
and the program's, and a second late errs the same way).  After: **0 of
20** by hand at no load.

**The fixture was also wrong, in the other direction.**  Under the
shake (eight burners on eight cores) the burst test failed **20 of 20**
after the fix — and the ticks said why: the burster got ~10 ticks a
second.  It burned 0.3 s of *wall* time a cycle, so on a loaded box it
was 10 % of a core, and under half a core-second in 2 s is idle by the
rule's letter: not a defect, a fixture that did not build the side it
meant (the 08-26 rule, `board/README.md`).  It now burns 0.3 s of CPU
a cycle (`time.process_time`), so its 30 % is 30 % on any box, and says
`idle 4`, so no tick of the clock is half its window: **0 of 10** under
the shake.  That test keeps the window claim; it no longer gates the
clock, which is why the gate above exists.

**The gate builds the defect's side deterministically**: a `date` on
PATH that returns 3 s more on every read, a program that sleeps a
second and then burns, `idle 3`.  On HEAD's launcher the first tick
reads as three seconds of silence and the program is stopped before it
has burned at all (measured: `idle: nothing has pulled skipnode for 3s`
after 2 s); on this one the third tick finds two seconds of burning
(measured: ran to its own exit at 6 s).  Load-proof: a full burn has
half a core-second by tick 3 at a quarter of a core.  Its first shake
was 9 of 10 — my own last assertion, not the stop: the ticks line is
written before the tick's own busy update, so the since-busy column
*reaches* IDLE on the tick that resets it; `≤`, not `<`.  Second shake,
0 of 10.
