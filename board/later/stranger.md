## What it is

The stranger test is the tree's own definition of "easy on the user
and understandable", written on day one and never run.  The person
in it has three things to do — start a program, see it stop when the
pulling stops, find out what it did — and is told nothing.  This card
is the test made ready, not run: what the stranger would be handed,
what counts as passing each of the three, and what the tree already
knows would fail.

## What the test is, ready to run

**The person.**  Someone who has used a terminal and has not read this
repository — not Henri, and not a session that has been in it.  A
session on a model that has never seen the tree is the cheapest
stranger and a poor one: it reads faster than a person and complains
less.  A person is the test; a cold session is the rehearsal.

**What they are handed.**  The repository, and nothing said.  Whether
the root `README.md` is enough is the first thing the test measures.

**The three acts, and what passes each.**

1. *Start a program.*  They find the llm node, or any node, and it
   runs.  Passes if a pull happens and the runner comes up without a
   question to anyone.  Known today to fail on a fresh machine: the
   loader path, the key file, `--jinja`, the install's `sudo` — each
   discovered by Henri by trying, each now written down somewhere,
   none of them on the path a stranger would walk.
2. *See it stop when they stop pulling.*  Passes if they can say why
   it stopped, in the tree's words, from what the tree showed them —
   `idle`, `sitting`, a cord.  Known today: the stop is one line in
   `llm/state/stopped` and one row on a panel they would have to know
   to open.
3. *Find out what it did.*  Passes if they find the account of a turn
   and the proposal it wrote, and can tell the two apart from tree
   content.  Known today: the banner says so on every file, which is
   the one part built for a stranger from the start.

**What is counted.**  Per act, the minutes to the first success and
the number of times they ask a question out loud — the andon's own
measure turned on the person.  Three acts, two numbers each, and the
list of every place they stopped and read something that did not
help.  That last list is the deliverable; the numbers only say how
long it was.

## What would make this card wrong

If the tree is not for strangers at all — a workbench for one person
and the sessions he runs, where "easy on the user" means easy on
*him*.  `vision.md` says otherwise in its first section, and he wrote
the priority in the plural ("the user"), so the card stands on his
words; but if the answer is "me first, strangers later", the test is
the same and the person is him after a week away, which is the
cheapest honest stranger there is.
